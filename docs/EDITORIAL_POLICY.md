# Editorial & Legal Accuracy Policy

## Evidence ladder
Every factual claim used in narration or on screen should be classified internally as one of:

1. **Court ruling / official record** — strongest available source.
2. **Prosecution allegation / referral record** — present as allegation, not neutral fact.
3. **Defense statement** — explicitly attributed.
4. **Credible reporting** — identify outlet/date and corroborate material claims.
5. **Background / social-media material** — use cautiously and never as sole proof of a serious allegation.
6. **Dramatization** — never evidence; purely illustrative.

## Language rules
Prefer precise formulations:
- `اتهمت النيابة...`
- `بحسب أمر الإحالة...`
- `قضت محكمة جنايات القاهرة في 5 سبتمبر 2026...`
- `قال الدفاع إنه سيتقدم باستئناف...`

Avoid collapsing procedural stages into a final statement such as `انتهت القضية` while appeal/review remains available or pending.

## Reconstruction rules
Generated scenes must be clearly differentiated from archival footage by at least one persistent visual indicator:
- `إعادة تمثيل` bug;
- distinct grade/film treatment;
- chapter-level disclosure where appropriate.

Never generate a photorealistic scene of a real identifiable person committing a crime and present it as captured footage. Use generic performers, silhouettes, partial-body framing, locations, objects, and abstract evidence visualizations instead.

## Sensitive material
Do not show graphic sexual-assault reenactments, humiliating imagery of victims, or unnecessary details. Summarize court allegations with restraint.

## Copyright
The reference YouTube video is for analysis. The production must be independently written and edited. Do not reuse its narration, soundtrack, or long unlicensed extracts.
