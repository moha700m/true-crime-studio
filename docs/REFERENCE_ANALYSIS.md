## What the reference does well
- Starts with stakes/shock before biography.
- Presenter is the stable narrative anchor.
- Intercuts presenter with reenactment, archival television, travel/location imagery, documents, and title cards.
- Uses questions and withheld answers to bridge chapters.
- Maintains visual novelty with frequent cutaways rather than leaving the presenter on screen continuously.
- Uses a dark studio, warm practical lights, a desk microphone, map imagery, props, and black negative space to establish a consistent world.
- Inserts short typographic cards for emphasis.

## Visual fingerprint observed
- Dark black/amber studio.
- Medium presenter framing at desk.
- Warm practicals and vintage props.
- White Arabic subtitles.
- Black title cards with selective red emphasis.
- Cinematic AI reenactments for unavailable events.
- B-roll of offices, travel/aviation, urban environments, documents, police/investigative motifs, and character stand-ins.

## What this project changes
We are not cloning the reference. Our visual system should be more evidentiary and premium:
- charcoal/black base;
- restrained oxblood red and archival amber;
- documentary typography;
- source/date bugs;
- timeline cards;
- evidence-board diagrams;
- court-procedure state cards;
- explicit reconstruction labels;
- stronger separation between `archive`, `reporting`, `court record`, and `reconstruction`.

## Retention pattern to preserve at a high level
`Shock → unanswered question → rewind → identity → escalation → network → break in the system → raid/investigation → evidence → court → unresolved/current legal status.`

This pattern is generic storytelling structure and should be rewritten around verified Sarah Khalifa facts rather than copied scene-for-scene.
